Practical diagnostics

We find the cause of the heating problem.

Heating problems are not always caused by the part you expect. A system may stop heating because of a thermostat setting, blower problem, control board, reversing valve, electrical fault, airflow restriction, or another issue inside the system.

Our technicians check the equipment, find the cause, and explain what needs to be repaired.

A heating system has several parts that need to work together. When a homeowner says the heat is not working, we do not assume the entire system has failed. We check the equipment and follow the problem until we understand what is happening.

We have also found heating problems tied to blower motors, thermostats, airflow restrictions, and system controls. The goal is to repair the problem that is actually affecting your system.

Heating troubleshooting

Common heating problems we diagnose and repair.

Heat Is Not Coming On

If nothing happens when you raise the thermostat, the problem may involve the thermostat, electrical power, a control board, blower motor, safety device, or another system component.

Our technicians check where the heating sequence is stopping so we can determine what needs attention.

Heat Pump Is Running but the House Is Still Cold

Problems with refrigerant levels, airflow, controls, the reversing valve, or other parts of the system can reduce heating performance.

Our technicians have worked on heat pumps with low refrigerant, control problems, reversing-valve concerns, and systems that were not operating correctly during heating mode.

We check how the complete system is operating instead of judging it only by whether the outdoor unit is running.

Blower Fan Is Not Working

Our work records include blower motors that hummed but would not start, motors with failed windings, loose electrical connections, and motors that needed replacement.

We check the motor, capacitor, wiring, and power going to the blower to determine why it is not working.

Heat Pump Keeps Shutting Down or Locking Out

Heat pumps use controls to manage heating, cooling, and defrost operation. Our technicians have diagnosed systems that entered lockout because they were not operating correctly during the heating and defrost cycle.

A system that keeps resetting, stopping, or displaying an error should be checked before the problem gets worse.

Auxiliary or Emergency Heat Is Running Too Much

Heat pumps may use electric auxiliary heat when additional heating is needed. If auxiliary heat runs too often, the home may still stay warm, but the system can use much more electricity.

The cause can depend on the heat pump, thermostat, outdoor equipment, and how the system is configured.

Our technicians work with heat pumps, electric backup heat, and dual-fuel systems and can check whether the heating equipment is operating the way it should.

Furnace Is Running Too Hot

A furnace that gets too hot may shut itself down for safety. Our service records include a gas furnace that was running hot because airflow was restricted by a dirty screen before the heat exchanger.

The technician cleaned and serviced the furnace and also checked the high-limit control, return airflow, and thermostat setup. Airflow is an important part of safe furnace operation, so overheating should not be ignored.

Thermostat or Control Problems

Sometimes the heating equipment is capable of working, but the controls are keeping it from operating correctly.

Our team has serviced and replaced thermostats, corrected thermostat wiring and programming, reset controls, and worked with thermostats controlling heat pumps, gas furnaces, electric heat, and dual-fuel systems.

A thermostat problem can look like an equipment failure, which is why it should be checked as part of the full system.
