Understanding the equipment

How heat pumps work.

A heat pump uses refrigerant to move heat instead of creating heat the same way a furnace does.

During the summer, it works much like a central air conditioner by moving heat out of the home.

During colder weather, the process reverses and the system pulls heat from outside and moves it indoors.

That means the same outdoor unit plays a major role in both heating and cooling.

Many heat pump systems also have electric backup heat. Some homes use dual-fuel systems that combine a heat pump with another heat source.

Our work records include heat pumps with electric heat, dual-fuel controls, air handlers, and systems that needed troubleshooting in both heating and cooling modes.

System-wide diagnostics

Heat pump repair and troubleshooting.

Heat pumps have many of the same parts as a central AC system, but they also have added controls that allow the system to switch between heating and cooling.

When a heat pump is not working correctly, our technicians may check:

  • Refrigerant pressures
  • Capacitors
  • Blower and fan motors
  • Electrical connections
  • Thermostat operation
  • Reversing valve operation
  • Defrost controls
  • Control boards
  • Coils
  • Air filters
  • Airflow
  • Electric backup heat
  • Compressor operation

Our work history includes heat pumps that would not cool because of failed capacitors, systems with low refrigerant, units that entered lockout during defrost, blower motor failures, and systems with possible reversing valve or TXV problems.

That is why a heat pump problem should be diagnosed before a repair is recommended. Learn more about our approach to heating repair and AC repair.

Helpful answers

Heat pump FAQs.

How do I know if I have a heat pump?

A heat pump often looks similar to a standard central AC system.

One clue is that the outdoor unit runs during both the summer and winter. Your thermostat may also have settings for auxiliary or emergency heat.

If you are not sure what type of system you have, we can identify the equipment during a service visit.

Why is my heat pump running but not heating well?

Several problems can reduce heat pump performance.

Low refrigerant, poor airflow, dirty coils, control issues, reversing valve problems, or other component failures can affect how much heat the system provides.

Our technicians have worked on heat pumps with refrigerant problems, defrost issues, control problems, and other faults that affected system performance.

What is auxiliary heat?

Auxiliary heat is a backup heat source that helps the heat pump when more heating is needed.

Many systems use electric heat strips inside the air handler.

Our service history includes systems where technicians checked electric heat operation and confirmed that the heat kit was working properly.

Why does my heat pump go into defrost mode?

A heat pump may need to remove frost from the outdoor coil during cold weather.

The system uses a defrost cycle to do this.

Our records include a heat pump that was short cycling during heating and entering defrost in a way that caused the control board to lock out system operation.

If the system keeps shutting down or seems stuck in defrost, it should be checked.

What does a reversing valve do?

The reversing valve allows a heat pump to switch between heating and cooling.

If there is a problem with the valve or the controls around it, the system may not change modes correctly.

Our technicians have checked reversing valve operation while diagnosing systems that were not heating or cooling as expected.

Should I repair or replace my heat pump?

Many heat pump problems can be repaired.

A bad capacitor, thermostat, motor, or smaller electrical issue may not justify replacing the system.

A failed compressor, leaking coil, repeated refrigerant problem, or several major failures may make replacement worth considering.

Our work records include customers who were given both repair and replacement options when a major heat pump repair was needed.

How often should my heat pump be serviced?

Because a heat pump provides both heating and cooling, it is used throughout much of the year.

For many Roanoke homeowners, having it checked before cooling season and again before heating season can make sense.

The right schedule can depend on the age of the system, how heavily it is used, and whether it has a history of problems.

Can a heat pump work with another type of heat?

Yes.

Our technicians have worked with dual-fuel systems that combine heat pumps with another heat source, including equipment supported through our furnace services, as well as systems that use electric backup heat.

The thermostat and controls need to be set up correctly so the different heat sources operate when they should.
