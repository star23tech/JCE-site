Repair or replacement

When should you replace a heating system?

Not every heating problem means you need a new system.

Many repairs involve smaller parts such as thermostats, capacitors, motors, contactors, wiring, or controls. In those cases, repairing the existing system may still make sense.

Other problems are more serious.

Our service records include systems with failed compressors, leaking coils, bad TXVs, major control problems, and older equipment where replacement was recommended. In one case, a customer was given a price for a major repair and a price for a new heat pump. The customer chose the new system because the replacement cost was close enough to the repair cost to make replacement the better option.

We look at the whole system before making that recommendation.

From evaluation to testing

Our heating replacement process.

01

Inspect the Existing System

The first step is finding out what is wrong with the current equipment. Depending on the system, our technicians may check:

  • Thermostat operation
  • Blower motors
  • Electrical components
  • Heat pump operation
  • Refrigerant pressures
  • Coils
  • Compressors
  • Airflow
  • Heat strips
  • Control boards
  • Reversing valves
  • Furnace controls

This helps us understand whether the system can still be repaired or whether replacement should be considered.

02

Review Repair and Replacement Options

If a repair is practical, we can explain that option.

If the system has a major failure or several problems at the same time, we can also discuss replacement.

Our records include heat pumps where major components were failing, systems with repeated refrigerant issues, and equipment where the repair cost was high enough that a new system became the better investment.

The decision should be based on the condition of the equipment, not just its age.

03

Choose the Right Type of Heating System

Roanoke homes use several types of heating equipment.

Some homes use heat pumps. Others have gas furnaces, electric heat, or dual-fuel systems.

Our work history includes heat pumps, natural gas furnaces, electric backup heat, air handlers, and dual-fuel systems.

We look at the existing setup and the needs of the home before recommending replacement equipment.

04

Remove the Old Equipment and Install the New System

Once the new equipment is selected, the old system is removed and the replacement is installed.

Our work history includes complete heat pump replacements, air handler work, mini split change-outs, coil replacements, compressor replacements, and new indoor and outdoor equipment.

The exact installation depends on the type of heating system in the home.

05

Test the New System

Installation is not complete until the new equipment has been tested.

Our technicians regularly document checking system operation after installation and repair. Depending on the equipment, this may include checking temperatures, electrical operation, airflow, refrigerant performance, thermostat control, and heating operation.

The system should be operating correctly before the job is finished.

Helpful answers

Heating replacement FAQs.

How do I know if I should repair or replace my heating system?

The answer depends on what has failed and the condition of the rest of the equipment.

A thermostat, capacitor, motor, or smaller electrical problem may be worth repairing. A failed compressor, leaking coil, repeated refrigerant problem, or several major failures may make replacement more practical.

We inspect the equipment before recommending either option.

How old does my heating system need to be before I replace it?

Age is only one part of the decision.

An older system that still runs well may not need replacement. A newer system with a major failure may still need to be evaluated.

We look at the condition of the equipment, repair cost, past problems, and how well the system is heating the home.

Should I replace my heat pump or repair it?

Many heat pump problems can be repaired.

Our technicians work on capacitors, motors, reversing valves, controls, refrigerant problems, TXVs, and other heat pump components.

If the heat pump has a major compressor failure, leaking coil, or other expensive problem, replacement may make more sense.

Can I replace a furnace with a heat pump?

That depends on the home and the existing heating system.

Our records include a homeowner who had a gas furnace and later requested a quote for a heat pump with an air handler and electric heat.

A change like this should be reviewed carefully because the electrical system, ductwork, home layout, and existing equipment may all affect the installation.

Do I need to replace both the indoor and outdoor equipment?

Sometimes.

A heat pump or central heating and cooling system often has indoor and outdoor equipment that work together.

The condition and compatibility of both parts should be considered before replacing only one section of the system.

We inspect the existing setup and explain what needs to be replaced.

What is a dual-fuel heating system?

A dual-fuel system uses two heat sources.

Our work records include systems that use a heat pump along with backup gas or oil heat. We have also worked with thermostats and controls that manage dual-fuel operation.

These systems need the right controls and setup so each heat source operates when it should.

Can you replace a system before it completely fails?

Yes.

If your heating system needs frequent repairs, has a major problem developing, or no longer heats the home well, it can be better to discuss replacement before the system stops working completely.

Planning ahead gives you more time to review your options instead of making a rushed choice during cold weather.

How do I know what type of new heating system is right for my home?

There is no single system that is right for every Roanoke home.

The existing equipment, ductwork, electrical service, fuel source, home layout, and comfort needs all matter.

John Calhoun Electric & HVAC works with several types of heating equipment, so we can review the existing system and help you understand the available options.
