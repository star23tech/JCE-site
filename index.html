ROANOKE ELECTRICAL & HVAC FAQS.

Many HVAC problems can be fixed with smaller repairs such as a capacitor, motor, drain cleaning, wiring repair, or control replacement. If the system has a failed compressor, leaking coil, repeated refrigerant loss, or several major problems, replacement may make more sense. The best choice starts with a full system check.

There are several possible causes. The system may be low on refrigerant, have a dirty coil, poor airflow, a bad capacitor, a clogged drain, a control problem, or a compressor issue. A proper diagnostic visit helps find the real cause before parts are replaced.

A breaker may trip because of an overloaded circuit, damaged wiring, a short, bad equipment, or another problem on the circuit. If the same breaker keeps tripping, it should be checked instead of being reset over and over.

Many AC systems have a safety switch that shuts the equipment off if water backs up in the drain pan. This helps prevent water damage. A clogged drain can make it look like the AC has failed even when the main cooling equipment is still working.

If a system is low on refrigerant, there may be a leak somewhere in the system. Adding refrigerant may help for a while, but it may not solve the real problem. The system should be checked to see why the refrigerant level is low.

Depending on the problem, a technician may check refrigerant pressures, supply and return temperatures, capacitors, voltage, motors, coils, filters, drains, float switches, thermostats, controls, and error codes. The goal is to find the cause of the problem instead of guessing.
