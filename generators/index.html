Choose what matters most

What do you want to keep running?

Some homeowners want backup power for a focused group of essential circuits. Others want a larger system designed to support most or all of the home. Planning starts by identifying the equipment that matters during an outage.

  • Heating equipment
  • Refrigerators and freezers
  • Sump pumps
  • Well pumps
  • Lighting
  • Selected outlets
  • Internet and communications
  • Other important household circuits

A system supporting a refrigerator, furnace, sump pump, and several lights has different requirements from one intended to carry most of the home's electrical demand. Reviewing the actual loads helps define the right system.

More than the generator itself

Backup power needs the right electrical setup.

A permanently installed generator works as part of the home's larger electrical system. Safe operation depends on properly selected transfer equipment, breakers, wiring, and coordination with the existing panel and service.

In one service call, a bedroom and closet had lost power but the problem was not in either room. Troubleshooting led to a tripped breaker inside the generator transfer equipment. Once the breaker was identified and reset, power was restored.

That experience illustrates why generator-related power trouble must be traced through the complete electrical system.

Generator FAQs

Answers to common backup-power questions.

What size generator do I need?

The right capacity depends on the circuits and equipment you want to power. The electrical loads should be reviewed before a generator is selected.

What is a whole-home generator?

It is a permanently installed backup generator designed to supply a large portion of a home's electrical system. How much it runs depends on the generator size and what you tie to it.

What does transfer equipment do?

It manages how the home changes between utility and generator power and helps prevent both sources from feeding the electrical system at the same time.

Can a generator power my heating and cooling system?

Possibly. HVAC pulls a lot. The generator and the wiring have to be sized for the exact equipment you want running.

Can I power only selected circuits?

Yes, and most people do. You pick the priorities. Heat, the refrigerator, the pumps, some lighting and outlets. New connections use the same dedicated-circuit work as any other equipment.

Does a standby generator connect to my panel?

Yes. A permanently installed generator integrates with the home's electrical system through properly installed transfer equipment and wiring. The existing panel and service are reviewed during planning.

How often should a generator be serviced?

Follow the manufacturer's service requirements. The appropriate schedule also depends on the generator and how often it operates. Regular checks help find problems before an outage.

Can you troubleshoot an existing generator system?

Yes. Generator trouble usually lands on breakers, the transfer switch, wiring, or controls. We inspect the electrical side and tell you what needs work.
