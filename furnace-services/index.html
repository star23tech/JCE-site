System-wide diagnostics

Furnace repair and troubleshooting.

Furnace trouble starts in a few different places. Our broader heating repair service follows the same system-wide diagnostic approach.

A furnace will run and still not push warm air. It overheats when airflow is choked off. The thermostat is set or wired wrong. The blower motor quits. A safety control shuts the whole thing down.

Here is what we check:

  • Thermostat settings and wiring
  • Blower motor operation
  • Electrical connections
  • Air filters
  • Return airflow
  • Furnace controls
  • High-limit switches
  • Heat output
  • Safety operation
  • Dual-fuel controls when present

Our service records show why this matters.

In one furnace service call, the system was running too hot. The technician found a dirty screen before the heat exchanger that was restricting airflow. The furnace was cleaned and serviced, and the technician also checked the high-limit switch, return airflow, and thermostat settings.

In another call, a gas furnace was not operating correctly because the cabinet door had shifted and caused the system to shut down. The technician corrected the door, cleaned the condensate drain, and checked the furnace operation before leaving.

These examples show why testing the whole system matters before replacing parts.

Gas heating equipment

Gas furnace service.

Gas furnaces need proper airflow, controls, and safety operation.

Our work records include service on natural gas furnaces, including systems with airflow restrictions, thermostat setup issues, and high-temperature concerns.

A furnace that gets too hot, cycles often, or shuts itself down should be checked.

Bad airflow makes the furnace work harder and trips the safety controls. Dirty filters and blocked returns are the usual reason.

Furnace maintenance.

Regular furnace maintenance gives you a chance to find small problems before the coldest part of winter.

Our technicians have performed furnace service and maintenance that included checking system operation, cleaning equipment, reviewing airflow, and making sure the furnace was heating properly.

A maintenance visit covers:

  • Checking the thermostat
  • Inspecting the air filter
  • Checking blower operation

  • Inspecting electrical connections

Helpful answers

Furnace FAQs.

Why is my furnace running but the house is still cold?

Weak heat usually comes down to airflow, the blower, the thermostat, or the furnace itself.

A furnace makes plenty of heat and still leaves the house cold if the air is not moving.

Our technicians check the full system to determine where the problem is coming from.

Why does my furnace keep shutting off?

A furnace shuts down when a safety control catches a problem.

One common cause is overheating from poor airflow.

Our service records include a gas furnace that was running hot because of an airflow restriction. The technician cleaned the system and checked the high-limit control and return airflow.

A furnace that keeps shutting off should be checked instead of repeatedly reset.

Why is my furnace blower not working?

The blower motor moves warm air through the home.

Our technicians have diagnosed blower motors that hummed but would not start, motors with bad windings, loose electrical connections, and motors that needed replacement.

We check the motor, capacitor, wiring, and power to find out why the blower is dead.

Can a dirty filter cause furnace problems?

Yes.

A dirty filter chokes airflow and makes the furnace work harder.

It also runs the furnace hotter than it should.

Changing the filter is the cheapest maintenance there is.

Why is my furnace blowing air that does not feel very warm?

The cause depends on the type of system.

It comes back to airflow, the thermostat, or the heat source itself.

We check supply temperatures to see whether the furnace is making the heat it should.

What is a high-limit switch?

A high-limit switch is a safety control that helps protect the furnace from overheating.

If the furnace runs too hot, that switch shuts it off.

Bad airflow is the usual reason it gets that hot. Our technicians check both the safety control and the reason the system is overheating.

Can a furnace work with a heat pump?

Yes.

Some homes use a dual-fuel system that combines a heat pump with another heat source.

Our technicians have worked with dual-fuel heating systems and the thermostats and controls used to manage them.

These systems need to be set up correctly so each heat source operates when it should.

How often should I have my furnace serviced?

For most Roanoke homeowners, having the furnace checked once a year before heating season is a good starting point.

Fall is a good time to make sure the system is ready before colder weather arrives.

Older equipment and problem systems need looking at more often.
