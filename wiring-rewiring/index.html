Plan before the walls are finished

New-home wiring starts with the right layout.

A new home gives you the opportunity to decide where power will be needed from the beginning. Plan the circuits and outlets early. It is how you avoid a bedroom with one receptacle and a kitchen circuit that trips every Thanksgiving.

  • Interior and exterior lighting
  • Standard receptacles
  • Kitchen appliances
  • Bathrooms
  • Laundry equipment
  • HVAC systems
  • Garage power
  • Sump pumps and dehumidifiers
  • Dedicated equipment
  • Future upgrades such as EV charging

We work with the circuits, wiring, breakers, outlets, switches, lighting, and equipment connections that form a complete residential electrical system.

Updating an existing home

Older wiring should be evaluated—not assumed.

Many older Roanoke homes were built for very different electrical needs. A modern kitchen, a heat pump, and a home office ask far more of a house than its original wiring ever planned for.

Rewiring means new conductors, corrected connections, added circuits, and bringing the system up to what the house needs now.

In one house where receptacles in several rooms had stopped working, electrical troubleshooting revealed multiple wiring problems throughout the property. Understanding what was already behind the walls was the first step toward planning the necessary corrections.

Reasons to inspect wiring Repeated electrical problems Loose or damaged connections Circuits frequently losing power Improperly installed wiring A system that no longer meets the home's needs

Wiring and rewiring FAQs

Answers to common home-wiring questions.

How do I know whether my home needs rewiring?

No single symptom automatically means a home needs complete rewiring. Repeated trouble, damaged wiring, loose connections, circuits losing power, or a system that no longer meets the home's needs are reasons to schedule an inspection.

Does an older home always need to be completely rewired?

No. Some homes need only certain circuits or sections replaced, while others require broader work. We evaluate the wiring's condition and the home's electrical needs before recommending the scope.

Can you rewire only part of a house?

Yes. Partial rewiring is often completed for kitchens, bathrooms, basements, additions, and other renovations when the remaining system is in suitable condition.

Can you add outlets and circuits during a rewire?

Yes. Rewiring is a good time to add receptacles, lighting, dedicated circuits, and other residential electrical upgrades based on how you use the home.

Will the walls need to be opened?

Sometimes. Attics, crawl spaces, and basements usually give us a path. Some areas mean opening a wall. The home's construction and wiring routes determine how much access is needed.

Can you correct wiring installed improperly?

Yes. We trace the circuits and correct loose connections, damaged conductors, backwired receptacles, and unsafe work somebody else left behind.

Does rewiring include replacing the panel?

Not automatically. The wiring and panel are related but separate. We look at the panel condition and capacity while we plan, so you know up front whether it stays or needs a panel replacement.

Can you wire a new home from the ground up?

Yes. We provide new-construction residential wiring as well as rewiring and electrical upgrades for existing homes.
