Understanding the system

What is an outdoor electrical service?

Your outdoor service connects the utility power supply to the electrical system inside your home. The exact arrangement varies by property, but it can include:

  • Service conductors
  • Meter equipment
  • Outdoor service wiring
  • Utility-to-panel connections
  • Outdoor disconnects
  • Other service equipment

These components must safely carry the electrical load used throughout the home. Damage can cause partial power loss, unreliable service, overheating, and other concerns that may require electrical troubleshooting to isolate.

Look beyond the immediate symptom

Restoring power is not always the same as fixing the service.

At one property that had lost one leg of power, our electrician found significant corrosion and poor conditions around the meter equipment. The existing underground service wiring was also a concern, and a larger repair needed to be planned.

On another project, a meter box was in serious disrepair. Although power could be restored temporarily, the condition of the equipment still called for a larger replacement project.

When the equipment bringing power into a home is badly deteriorated, replacing one component or temporarily restoring power may not correct the underlying problem.

What does “one leg” mean? A home normally receives power through more than one energized conductor. If one is lost, some circuits may work while others have no power. Partial power loss should be inspected promptly.

Outdoor service FAQs

Answers to common service replacement questions.

How do I know whether the outdoor service needs replacement?

Signs can include partial power loss, visible corrosion, damaged meter equipment, unreliable incoming power, or equipment in poor physical condition. An inspection determines whether a repair is enough or replacement is needed.

Is outdoor service replacement the same as panel replacement?

No. The outdoor service brings utility power into the property; the panel distributes that power to circuits inside the home. Some projects affect one, while others require work on both. Learn more about electrical panel replacement.

Does the utility need to disconnect my power?

Some projects require the electric utility to disconnect power before service components can be safely replaced and reconnect it when the work is ready. The requirement depends on the work's type and location.

Will the project require an electrical permit?

Outdoor service replacement may require electrical permits and inspections. We determine the applicable requirements and coordinate them with the project schedule.

Can corroded service equipment be repaired?

It depends on the location and extent of the damage. A small, isolated issue may be repairable, while heavy corrosion or badly deteriorated equipment can require replacement.

What if the service wiring is underground?

Underground service wiring can still deteriorate or develop faults. Its condition and installation must be evaluated along with the meter and other service equipment before replacement or broader wiring work is planned.

Can the service be replaced before it fails completely?

Yes. Addressing badly corroded or damaged equipment before a complete failure allows time to plan permitting and utility coordination instead of handling the entire project during an outage.
