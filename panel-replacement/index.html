Repair or replace?

Not every breaker problem requires a new panel.

A tripping breaker does not automatically mean the panel has failed. The cause may be one damaged breaker, a loose connection, an overloaded circuit, faulty wiring, or equipment elsewhere in the electrical system. We troubleshoot the problem first and recommend replacement when the condition or capacity of the system calls for it.

At one property with a partial loss of power, our electrician found heavy corrosion and poor conditions around the meter equipment, along with concerns about the underground service wiring. The issue required planning for a larger electrical service repair—not simply changing a breaker.

On another project, damaged breakers and internal components in a pool subpanel could be replaced without replacing the entire electrical service. Inspecting the full system helps distinguish a focused repair from a project that genuinely needs replacement.

More room for current and future circuits.

Electrical panel FAQs

Answers to common panel replacement questions.

How do I know whether my panel needs replacement?

Warning signs can include corrosion, visible heat damage, recurring breaker trouble, partial power loss, or inadequate capacity for the home's electrical needs. An inspection is the best way to determine whether repair or replacement is appropriate.

Can you replace only one bad breaker?

Yes, when the breaker is the problem and the panel is otherwise safe and in good condition. We inspect the equipment before deciding whether a smaller repair is sufficient.

What if there is corrosion in the panel or service equipment?

Corrosion should be evaluated promptly. The proper repair depends on its location, extent, and whether it has damaged the panel, meter equipment, connections, or other service components.

Will the power need to be shut off?

Yes. The equipment must be safely de-energized during replacement. Some projects also require the electric utility to disconnect and reconnect service power.

Does panel replacement require a permit?

Panel and service work may require electrical permits, inspections, and utility coordination. We determine which requirements apply to the specific project.

Is panel replacement the same as service replacement?

Not necessarily. The panel is one part of the system; the electrical service may also include meter equipment, service conductors, and other components that bring power into the home. We explain which parts need attention after inspection.
