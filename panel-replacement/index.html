Repair or replace?

Not every breaker problem requires a new panel.

A tripping breaker does not mean the panel is bad. It is often one worn breaker, a loose connection, or a circuit with too much on it. We find the cause first. We tell you to replace the panel when the panel is really the problem.

One house had lost power to half its rooms. We opened it up and found heavy corrosion around the meter and trouble in the underground service wire. That was an electrical service repair, not a breaker swap.

Another job looked worse than it was. A pool subpanel had burned breakers and damaged parts inside, but the main service was fine. We rebuilt the subpanel and left the rest alone.

Electrical panel FAQs

Answers to common panel replacement questions.

How do I know whether my panel needs replacement?

Look for rust, scorch marks, breakers that trip over and over, or rooms that lose power. A panel with no open slots left is another sign. We inspect it to know for sure.

Can you replace only one bad breaker?

Yes, if the breaker is the problem and the panel is sound. We check the panel first.

What if there is corrosion in the panel or service equipment?

Get it looked at soon. What it takes to fix depends on where the rust is and how far it has gone into the panel or the meter.

Will the power need to be shut off?

Yes. The panel has to be dead while we work. On some jobs the power company pulls the meter and resets it afterward.

Does panel replacement require a permit?

Most panel and service work does. We pull the permit and handle the inspection.

Is panel replacement the same as service replacement?

No. The panel is one piece of it. The service also takes in the meter and the wires feeding the house. Sometimes only one of them needs work.
