Why maintenance matters

Many AC problems start small.

A drain starts to clog. A coil packs with dirt. A capacitor weakens. Refrigerant drifts off where it should be. A loose connection works itself worse.

Our work records show poor cooling and shutdowns tied to dirty coils, clogged drains, bad filters, weak capacitors, and low refrigerant. Regular maintenance gives us a chance to look for those problems before the system stops working on a hot day.

That means fewer surprise calls and a better shot at getting through August without one.

A thorough system check

What we check during an AC tune-up.

Outdoor Coil

We inspect the outdoor coil and clean it so the system can dump heat.

Indoor Coil

We check the evaporator coil for dirt and buildup that chokes airflow.

Condensate Drain and Trap

We check the drain and trap so water runs off instead of tripping the float switch.

Air Filter and Airflow

We check filter condition, installation, and signs of restricted airflow that make the system work harder.

Refrigerant Operation

We check how the system runs and watch for refrigerant that is off.

Electrical Components

We inspect capacitors, contactors, wiring, motors, and controls for wear, looseness, damage, or abnormal operation.

Motors and Moving Parts

We check indoor blowers and outdoor fans and listen for unusual noise or poor performance.

Supply and Return Temperatures

Temperature readings help show whether the system is removing heat from the home as expected.

Overall System Operation

We confirm that indoor and outdoor equipment starts, runs, and shuts down properly and look for other repair needs.

Typical maintenance visit

What is included.

The exact steps vary depending on the equipment and what we find during the visit.

  • Inspect and clean the condenser coil
  • Inspect the evaporator coil
  • Check the air filter
  • Check the condensate drain and trap
  • Test the float switch when present
  • Check refrigerant operation
  • Inspect capacitors and contactors
  • Check electrical connections
  • Check blower and fan motors
  • Check voltage and amp draw when needed
  • Check supply and return temperatures
  • Test thermostat operation
  • Run the full cooling system

Recommended frequency

Once a year

For most Roanoke homeowners, ideally before the hottest part of summer.

Spring is a good time to prepare the system before daily heavy use.

Maintenance timing

How often should your AC be serviced?

Older equipment, hard use, pets, and a history of drain trouble all mean you want it looked at more often.

A heat pump runs all year, so it earns two visits. Once before cooling season and again before heating season. Learn more about our heat pump services.

If we turn up a problem, we tell you whether it is an AC repair or, on older equipment with a major failure, time to talk about AC installation and replacement.
